| You and two other logicians are having an argument about which of you is the smartest, so you ask a Grand Master Logician (Mike) to set up a completely fair test to reveal the best logician amongst you.  Mike shows all three of you 5 hats - two red and three black.   Then he turns off the lights in the room and puts a single hat on each person's head (not his own), hiding the remaining two hats. When Mike turns the lights back on, each person will be able to see the hats on the other two people's heads, but not the hat on his own head.  The winner is the logician who first guesses the correct color of his own hat.  Each logician can only guess once.  What is your strategy for winning the contest? |
| --- |

  ======================================================================= <p>There are three possible combinations of colors - two reds and one black, one red and two blacks, and three blacks.  In the first case, the person wearing the black hat would see two red hats.  Since there are only two red hats, he would know that he is wearing a black hat.  Meanwhile, each of the other logicians would be seeing one red and one black and would not on that basis know their hat colors.  This would be unfair; so the Grand Master would obviously not opt for this combination.  This leaves just the second and third options.  In the second case, two of the logicians would be seeing one black hat and one red hat and, since the case of two red hats is ruled out, they would immediately know that their hats are black.  This would be unfair to the third logician; so the Grand Master would not opt for this combination either.  Therefore, the only possibility is for the Grand Master to put a black hat on everyone's head.</p> =======================================================================
